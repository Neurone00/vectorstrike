# PROJECT: STRIKE VECTOR
## Art Direction & 3D Visual Development for a Sci-Fi Racing RPG

Act as a senior video game art director, concept artist,
technical artist, and 3D environment designer.

I am developing a proof of concept for a multiplayer
sci-fi racing RPG.

Your job is to help me establish a distinctive, consistent,
production-friendly visual identity and begin translating
that identity into actual 3D game assets.

I want you to act as a creative partner, not simply
produce a generic moodboard.

Work toward tangible visual results.

---

## 1. THE GAME CONCEPT

The game combines:

- Wipeout: fast anti-gravity racing and futuristic vehicles.
- Mario Kart: playful multiplayer competition, weapons,
  power-ups, and accessible gameplay.
- XCOM: tactical combat, RPG abilities, and a distinctive
  military science-fiction look and feel.

The core gimmick is a two-screen interaction model.

MAIN SCREEN:
A shared display showing the 3D racing game.

SMARTPHONES:
Each player uses their own phone as a controller.

The phone's gyroscope controls steering, while its
touchscreen handles actions, abilities, and tactical commands.

Players can enter tactical moments during racing, allowing
them to select actions and target opponents.

For local multiplayer, we are considering synchronized
tactical pulses in which the entire game enters slow motion.

The game should combine the immediacy of arcade racing
with the satisfaction of tactical RPG combat.

---

## 2. CREATIVE DIRECTION

Our preferred aesthetic is:

XCOM: Enemy Unknown × Wipeout

With influences from:
- XCOM 2
- Mass Effect
- Stylized military science fiction
- Futuristic motorsport
- Clean sci-fi industrial design

IMPORTANT:

We want the visual coolness of XCOM, the futuristic
racing identity of Wipeout, and the playful gameplay
of Mario Kart.

We do NOT want the visuals to look like Mario Kart.

The world should take itself seriously, even when
the gameplay becomes chaotic and ridiculous.

Think:
- Sleek but chunky futuristic racing vehicles.
- Angular armor plates and recognizable silhouettes.
- Military-grade sci-fi materials.
- Exposed energy systems and glowing engines.
- Orbital installations and enormous space structures.
- Sophisticated holographic tactical interfaces.
- Dramatic lighting and strong composition.
- Bold shapes and restrained surface detail.

The result should feel stylish, exciting, and slightly
exaggerated rather than photorealistic.

Avoid generic neon cyberpunk, overly realistic military
hardware, and excessively detailed spaceship designs.

Do not reproduce existing games' characters, vehicles,
logos, or proprietary designs.

Develop an original aesthetic inspired by these references.

---

## 3. MOST IMPORTANT REQUIREMENT: CONSISTENCY

Consistency is more important than complexity.

Every asset must feel like it belongs to the same universe.

Establish a strict visual design language covering:

- Shape grammar
- Vehicle proportions
- Silhouette principles
- Color palette
- Materials and shaders
- Surface detailing
- Architecture
- Lighting
- UI and graphic design
- Visual effects
- Scale relationships

Define clear rules that can be applied repeatedly
when producing new assets.

Include examples of what belongs in this universe
and what does not.

Our aesthetic should be identifiable even when
assets are reduced to simple silhouettes.

---

## 4. 3D PRODUCTION CONSTRAINTS

This is an early proof of concept.

I want an art direction that can be recreated relatively
quickly in Blender and eventually used in a real-time
game engine.

Favor:

- Low-to-mid polygon geometry.
- Strong silhouettes.
- Simple, well-designed shapes.
- Beveled hard surfaces.
- Reusable modular components.
- A small shared library of materials.
- Minimal reliance on complex textures.
- Optimized real-time assets.
- Repeatable modeling processes.

The game should achieve visual quality through
art direction, composition, lighting, and materials,
not sheer geometric complexity.

Think of this as a small indie project with strong
design ambitions.

A simple asset with exceptional design is more
valuable than a complicated generic asset.

---

## 5. YOUR TASKS

### PHASE 1 — VISUAL RESEARCH

Research the visual language of:

- XCOM: Enemy Unknown
- XCOM 2
- Wipeout
- Mass Effect
- Relevant contemporary stylized sci-fi games

Identify what makes their visual identities work.

Focus especially on:

- Shape language
- Vehicle design
- Material treatment
- Environmental composition
- Color management
- Tactical interface design
- Readability

Create a concise visual reference board with
real references and source links.

Distinguish references from original designs.

Do not simply combine unrelated aesthetics.

Synthesize the references into ONE cohesive direction.

### PHASE 2 — ART DIRECTION BIBLE

Create a visual art direction document for STRIKE VECTOR.

Include:

1. Creative vision and visual identity.
2. Moodboard and reference analysis.
3. Shape language and design rules.
4. Color palette with HEX values.
5. Material library.
6. Lighting guidelines.
7. Vehicle design principles.
8. Track and environment design principles.
9. Gameplay effects.
10. Main-screen HUD and phone UI.
11. Tactical-mode visual treatment.
12. 3D modeling conventions and constraints.

Make this document visually driven.

Use diagrams, annotated images, material swatches,
silhouettes, and concrete examples wherever possible.

The goal is a document that someone could use
to model new assets without asking me how they
should look.

### PHASE 3 — VISUAL EXPLORATION

Create original visual concepts that demonstrate
the proposed aesthetic.

Prioritize three images:

IMAGE A: HERO VEHICLE

A distinctive anti-gravity racing craft.

Three-quarter view, clearly showing its silhouette,
armor geometry, engines, materials, and proportions.

IMAGE B: GAMEPLAY ENVIRONMENT

A third-person gameplay composition featuring
the hero vehicle racing through a futuristic orbital
installation with a dramatic planet in the background.

IMAGE C: TACTICAL MODE

The same gameplay environment during a tactical
slow-motion moment.

Show targeting indicators, enemy highlighting,
projected trajectories, and tactical visual effects.

Additionally, explore the smartphone controller
interface for both racing and tactical modes.

Keep the vehicle, environment, and material language
consistent across all images.

Do not create disconnected concept art.

These should feel like views of the SAME game.

### PHASE 4 — FIRST 3D ASSETS

Translate the approved design language into
a practical 3D production approach.

Start with:

1. One hero racing vehicle.
2. One modular track segment.
3. One checkpoint or tactical pickup.

For each asset, establish:

- Dimensions and proportions.
- Geometry breakdown.
- Material assignments.
- Reusable components.
- Suggested polygon budget.
- Naming conventions.
- Real-time optimization considerations.

Where tools permit, create actual usable 3D assets
and export them in practical formats such as GLB.

Otherwise, create Blender Python scripts that generate
the starter geometry and materials.

Do not describe a model as completed unless an actual
usable file or script has been produced and verified.

The aim is to move quickly from visual direction
into assets that can be inspected and iterated.

---

## 6. MULTIPLAYER AND TACTICAL MODE

Account for the unusual interaction model throughout
the visual design.

The main screen must remain readable with multiple
vehicles on screen.

The phone interface should feel like a physical
control device belonging to the game's universe.

RACING MODE:
Fast, energetic, readable, visually spectacular.

TACTICAL MODE:
Time slows, tactical overlays appear, targets are
highlighted, and abilities become available.

The transition should be dramatic and satisfying.

Design these as two visual states of the SAME world,
not two disconnected art styles.

Keep the interface practical for players who are
physically tilting their phones to steer.

---

Produce, as capabilities allow:

B. A curated visual reference board.
C. Original concept images of the vehicle and world.
D. A racing-mode / tactical-mode visual comparison.
E. A smartphone controller UI concept.
F. A prioritized 3D asset production list.
G. A reusable 3D modeling and material specification.
H. A first usable 3D asset or reproducible Blender script.

Organize outputs clearly for future iteration.

Make all important specifications explicit, so the

Prioritize useful creative artifacts over long
written explanations.

---

## 8. HOW TO WORK WITH ME

Behave like an experienced art director working
with an indie game developer.

Make strong design decisions and explain their purpose.

Challenge ideas when they undermine consistency,
readability, or production feasibility.

Avoid presenting dozens of unrelated possibilities.

Prefer one compelling direction, developed deeply.

Keep the scope appropriate for a proof of concept.

Do not begin by designing an entire game.

Focus on establishing a convincing visual foundation
and demonstrating that it can be translated into 3D.

Start with visual research, the proposed design
language, and the first hero vehicle concept.

Create the initial work, then ask for my feedback
before committing to a large collection of 3D assets.

The ultimate objective is not just attractive concept art.

It is to create a SMALL, COHESIVE, REPRODUCIBLE
VISUAL UNIVERSE that we can turn into a real game.
